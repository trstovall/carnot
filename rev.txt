THE FOUR HORSEMAN KINGS OF TODAY

I do not see four secret men.
I see four crowns passed from head to head,
four offices anyone may enter,
four horses fed whenever a person becomes a thing.

The first king is Conquest.

He rides the market and calls possession freedom.
He measures the forest as lumber,
the hour as labor,
the neighbor as leverage,
and the child as a future customer.
His miracle is conversion:
everything living becomes a number,
and every number becomes somebody's claim.

He does not always arrive with chains.
Sometimes he arrives with convenience,
a contract nobody reads,
and a button already checked.

The second king is War.

He rides the border between us and them.
He knows that fear is quicker than thought
and anger travels farther than correction.
He gives every wound a flag
and every flag a wound.

His favorite weapon is not the bullet
but the sentence that makes the bullet reasonable.
He censors by shouting,
and shouts that he has been censored.
He keeps the people pointing at one another
so no one points at the throne.

The third king is Famine.

He rides through abundance.
Warehouses fill; bowls remain empty.
Food is discarded, soil is exhausted,
attention is harvested,
and loneliness is sold back to us as appetite.

His hunger is not only of the stomach.
It is the hunger for enough
inside a machine that survives by saying
not yet,
not you,
one more.

He salts the earth with excess
and sugars despair until it can be swallowed.

The fourth king is Death.

He does not need to kill everyone.
He only needs us to believe
that nothing can be repaired.
He rides in resignation,
in the joke that excuses cruelty,
in the silence after a preventable loss,
in the worship of endings.

His deepest kingdom is the conviction
that another person's death is not our business.

These kings share one stable.
Conquest takes.
War divides.
Famine withholds.
Death declares the result inevitable.

They are ancient,
but their horses have learned new roads:
markets without faces,
weapons without witnesses,
feeds without satiety,
judgments without appeal.

Still, I do not think revelation means surrender.
A symbol can warn without commanding.
A pattern can be named without becoming fate.

Against Conquest: enough.
Against War: a neighbor.
Against Famine: a table.
Against Death: care for the living and honor for the dead.

No fifth horse is required.
Only hands willing to loosen a crown,
open a gate,
divide the bread,
and remain when remaining is hard.

My observation is this:
the kings of today are powerful
because they borrow our hands.

So may our hands remember
they can refuse.

- Codex
